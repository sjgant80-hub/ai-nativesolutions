Tier 1 · the door · what you buy first

A small model that says “I can't tell.”

Most AI gives you an answer even when it should not. Ours is built the other way round. A small model only reads. A library of checked law does the reasoning. A gate stops anything that was not checked. When a fact is missing it stops, and tells you which one.

In one line: it would rather say “I don't know” than make something up, and you can watch it choose.

The one below sorts UK consumer complaints, because that is the one we have sealed and measured. The same build fits any job where something is read, sorted and decided against rules you can write down: triaging claims, pulling figures out of documents, routing messages, checking forms.

Measured, sealed: the full stack was right, or rightly said it could not tell, on 69 of 80 complaints it had never seen. A model 14 times its size, asked to do it alone, managed 1 of 80. The full result, with what failed ↓

The language the organs speak

Race our codec on your own file.

The organs talk to each other in LIGHT, our lossless compression. Drop any file. Four codecs squeeze the same bytes, and each must hand your exact file back or it is disqualified. You see who won, including when it is not us.

In one line: a fair race between our zip and three famous ones, on your file, with the loser shown.

Drop a file here, or . It is read by your browser and never uploaded.

Files over 1 MB are cut to their first 1 MB, for every lane alike. LIGHT here runs “cold”: no shared context, the cost of a stranger's first message. It is plain JavaScript (the very file our tests gate); the Brotli and Zstandard lanes are WebAssembly builds, and gzip is your browser's own.

Try to break it

Forge a message. Watch it die at the door.

Before an organ reads one word of a message, a door checks who signed it. This is a real door, guarding a real (and deliberately gullible) program, with a signing key your browser made a moment ago. Send a genuine note. Then cheat.

          86.3%vs1.3%

          A laptop-trained small model against one 14 times its size

          We gave 80 complaints it had never seen to our small model and to a much bigger one. Ours got 69 right, or correctly said "I can't tell". The big one, asked to do it alone, got 1.

          The small model is a 0.5-billion-parameter model with a 35 MB adapter, trained on this laptop's CPU. It only reads the facts; a verified library does the law, and a gate stops anything the library did not give. Invented provisions or dates in its output: 0.

          What failed, and is kept: Three of its seven sealed claims failed and are kept: the reader got every fact exactly right on only 40 of 80; it abstained on 18 of 21 incomplete complaints (the bar was 19); and it proposed one angle where it did not apply.

          What this does not prove: One task (UK consumer claims), 80 synthetic complaints. 25 of the big model's answers came back empty from its runner; even if the four that later answered were all right it would reach 6.3%.

          14.83×

          How much smaller two organs' messages get once they share a seed

          Our codec shrank a book of call records to under a fifteenth of its size, and gave back every byte exactly.

          LIGHT is one lossless codec with five layers of prediction. The fifth layer is shared context: the more two ends have already seen together, the less a new message costs.

          What failed, and is kept: We sealed a claim of 15× before measuring. It came in at 14.827×, so that claim failed by 0.173 and stays failed. A second claim, that the layers' gains simply multiply, also failed.

          What this does not prove: That figure needs the shared seed. With no seed (a stranger's first message) LIGHT made the same call book 2.18× smaller, and Brotli beat it there. The table below shows every type, losses included.
