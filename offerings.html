The position

Software is going to zero. Own the system instead.

Renting software means paying forever for something you never come to own, with your data sitting on somebody else's hardware and your costs rising as you use it more.

The alternative is not cheaper software. It is owned software: built once, handed over with the source, running on machines you control. There is no vendor to be locked into, because after handover there is no vendor — there is your team and your code.

That means the relationship is build → transfer → leave. Anything recurring afterwards is advice you can stop, never access to what was delivered.

A two-week build sounds impossible if every project starts at an empty folder. These do not. Each engagement assembles pieces that already exist and have already been tested to destruction, and adds only the part specific to you.

Why it cannot simply be copied

Anyone can say local-first. Proving it is the hard part.

  • The library. A competitor starting from zero quotes months for what assembles here in weeks. That gap widens with every engagement.
  • Proof rather than claims. Every component's tests are run on neutral hardware, from a fresh copy, with no credentials — so the result is something you can reproduce, not something you have to take on faith.
  • It joins up. Systems delivered to different clients can be made to work together without either party giving up control of their own data.
  • One standard. A distributed team producing interchangeable, independently checkable work is what makes enterprise delivery possible without becoming a body shop.

Every delivery carries a verification you can run yourself, on your own hardware. Not a certificate somebody issued — a check you perform.
