What this does — and doesn’t — claim

It is narrow on purpose. It works where a computer can check the answer: sums, logic, and facts copied from a page. For the rest it says “I can’t tell.”

Copied is not the same as true. If a page is wrong, an answer copied from it is wrong too. We mark which answers are proved and which are copied.

The question box is small, and it slips. It knows short pieces of text: about the Normans, about oxygen, and about how hard some problems are. The twelve questions come from a public test set, spelled as that set spells them. In four of them ours is wrong. We kept those, and you can pick them.

Nothing is claimed early. The learning test is still running. Its score goes on this page when it is done, pass or fail.

Questions buyers ask

What do we get? A small AI for one job. The rules it works from. The checker that stops bad answers. And a test you can run again. All on your own computers.

What does it replace? The hours a person spends reading and sorting. And the software you rent to help them. It does not replace their judgement.

Where does our data go? Nowhere. The AI runs on your computers, with no link to the internet. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else.

What about the rules we must follow? Each answer says what it is based on. Each “can’t tell” says what was missing. So there is a record a person can check. Ask your own advisers if that meets the rules for your firm. We will show them anything they ask to see.

How do we roll it out? One job first. We test it against how the job is done today. Then the next job.

Who looks after it? AI Native Solutions does. The terms are agreed for each customer, in their own agreement. The code is yours to keep under the MIT licence.

What can go wrong? A small AI can misread a fact. The checker catches a date or a sum that is not in the text. It cannot catch a judgement call. So a person still reads each result.

What does it cost? You pay once, for the work. There is no monthly fee and no fee for each seat. The long paper for buyers shows the sums.
